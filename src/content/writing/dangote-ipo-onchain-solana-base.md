<section class="sec"><div class="fids"><span class="n">01</span><span class="t">What actually shipped</span><span class="r"></span></div>
<h3 class="nh">Two identical tranches, six minutes apart</h3>
<p class="lede">The Dangote Petroleum Refinery offer opened on the Nigerian Exchange on 14 September 2026: 4.1 billion ordinary shares at ₦525, a raise of about ₦2.15 trillion, closing 13 October with a listing in November. Most of the coverage described the crypto side as a way to pay with stablecoins. That undersells what was actually deployed.</p>
<p>On the morning the offer opened, an identical tranche of <b>380,952.38 DPRI</b> was minted on Solana and again on Base. That number is not arbitrary. At the ₦525 offer price it is exactly ₦200,000,000 per chain. The two deployments went live six minutes apart.</p>

<p>Three further Solana mints carry the same name and the same DPRI ticker, issued at 09:16, 09:17 and 09:38 that morning. All three have zero supply and have never moved a token. They are abandoned deployment attempts, and they matter only because anyone searching the ticker will find them sitting next to the live one.</p>
<p>One more correction to the public account. Subscribers are not paying in dollars. Settlement on both chains runs in <b>cNGN</b>, the naira stablecoin: mint <code>3jiqwBQVRC5zRwHyqvnkQurebJ5RNxg3F5fXMwaxgkv8</code> on Solana, contract <code>0x46c85152bfe9f96829aa94755d9f915f9b10ef5f</code> on Base. Naira in, naira denominated shares out. It is a domestic rail that happens to run on public infrastructure, not a dollar on ramp.</p>
</section>

<section class="sec"><div class="fids"><span class="n">02</span><span class="t">Take-up so far</span><span class="r"></span></div>
<h3 class="nh">Real, live, mirrored, and a rounding error</h3>
<p>Both tranches are open and both are barely touched. Solana has delivered 8,417 DPRI to 47 wallets, which is 2.21% of its ₦200m allocation. Base has delivered 3,456 to 31 wallets, 0.91%. Solana is running roughly two and a half times ahead of Base on the same asset, offered at the same price, on the same day.</p>

<p>Combined, that is <b>11,873 DPRI across 78 wallets, ₦6,233,404</b>. Set against a ₦2.15 trillion book it is 0.00029% of the offer. The honest framing is that the tokenised rail works, it is genuinely live on two chains, and so far almost nobody has used it.</p>
<p class="note">One caveat on reading any of this. The offer has four weeks left to run, so every figure here is a run rate on day one, not a result. What is worth watching is not the level but the shape, and the shape is already telling.</p>

<section class="sec"><div class="fids"><span class="n">03</span><span class="t">The settlement gap</span><span class="r"></span></div>
<h3 class="nh">Base is sixteen times worse at delivering the shares</h3>
<p>A subscription has two legs. cNGN goes in to the treasury, DPRI comes back out to the subscriber. Measuring only one of them is how two analysts looking at the same offer end up with different numbers, and the gap between the legs turns out to be the most interesting thing on either chain.</p>

<p>On Solana, ₦4,466,195 came in from 47 wallets and 8,417 of the 8,507 shares paid for have landed. That is <b>1.1% outstanding</b>, and every single wallet that paid is holding tokens.</p>
<p>On Base, ₦2,197,881 came in from 34 wallets but only 3,456 of the 4,186 shares paid for have been delivered. That is <span class="flag">17.4% outstanding, ₦383,302</span>, and only 31 of the 34 paying wallets hold anything at all. Three wallets have paid and received nothing back yet.</p>
<div class="callout"><p>Same issuer, same asset, same price, contracts deployed six minutes apart, and a sixteen fold difference in how reliably the shares arrive. Across both chains <b>₦430,672 is currently paid for and undelivered</b>.</p></div>
<p>Worth being careful about what this does and does not show. A batch settlement process that clears every few hours would produce exactly this picture on a young offer, in which case the gap closes on its own and means nothing. The Base treasury ledger also shows heavy churn, including a single 80,000 DPRI movement out and straight back in, which is consistent with operational batching rather than anything lost. The test is simply time. If the Base gap closes over the next few days it was plumbing. If it holds near 17% or widens while Solana stays near 1%, the two rails are not the same product, and anyone choosing between them should know that before they send naira.</p>
</section>

<section class="sec"><div class="fids"><span class="n">04</span><span class="t">The copycat market</span><span class="r"></span></div>
<h3 class="nh">146 times more money went to the impersonations</h3>

<p>The two largest copycats are both called Dangote Refinery, both ticker DANGR, and both were minted <b>before the offer opened</b>, on 12 and 13 September. Between them they account for 93.5% of that volume. Their trading also does not look like buying: 9,017 trades across 297 wallets on one, 8,311 trades across 82 wallets on the other, which works out at 30 and 101 trades per wallet. That pattern fits wash trading rather than demand, so the headline should be read as gross flow through these tokens, not as money that changed hands between real buyers, and certainly not as losses.</p>

<p>The timing is the clearest signal in the whole dataset. Solana had been producing roughly one Dangote named token a month for over a year. In the week the offer opened it produced fifteen, with another seven the week before as the news built.</p>
<p>Across every chain, <b>129 tokens</b> now carry the Dangote name: 68 on Base, 39 on Solana, 18 on the Robinhood chain, 3 on BNB and 1 on Cronos. Base holds the largest count by far, but those 68 have traded a cumulative $2,239 between 26 wallets across two years and are mostly auto minted content coins scraped from social posts. They have nothing to do with the offer. This is also not a Dangote specific problem: the same scan finds <b>5,731</b> tokens carrying the xStock name on Solana, almost all of them impersonating a tokenised equity brand.</p>

<div class="closer"><div class="q">The rail is the story, and the rail is unevenly built</div>
<p>A ₦2.15 trillion African IPO is genuinely settling shares on two public chains, in naira, with every subscription traceable by anyone who wants to check. That is a real milestone and it deserves to be reported as one. It is also, one day in, 0.00029% subscribed on-chain, delivering unevenly between the two chains it launched on, and surrounded by an impersonation market that has moved 146 times more money than the offer itself. All four of those things are true at once, and any account that gives you only the first or only the last is selling you something.</p></div>
