<h3 class="nh">Six quarters of a very steady number</h3>
<p class="lede">Read only the headline and Nigeria's economy had an uneventful year. Real GDP grew 4.23% in the second quarter of 2025 and 4.43% in the second quarter of 2026. In the six quarters from the start of 2025 it never left a band 1.3 points wide.</p>
<p>Most coverage stops there. I built a Power BI dashboard on the National Bureau of Statistics tables for Q2 2026 to find out what that steady number was made of. The answer is that almost nothing inside it held still.</p>

<p>Electricity and gas went from growing 11.5% to shrinking 10.6%, a swing of <b>22.1 points</b>. Transport and storage fell from 22.1% to 5.7%. Mining and quarrying, where crude oil sits, fell from 20.9% to 6.4%. Finance and insurance slowed from 16.1% to 9.3%.</p>

<section class="sec"><div class="fids"><span class="n">06</span><span class="t">How it was built</span><span class="r"></span></div>
<h3 class="nh">A dashboard that checks its own numbers against NBS</h3>
<p>The source is the NBS Q2 2026 GDP workbook, which holds level tables for all 46 economic activities across ten quarters, in current prices and in 2019 constant prices. I used only the levels and derived every growth rate, share, deflator and contribution in DAX, so no number on the dashboard is copied from a published table.</p>
<p>Deriving everything carries a risk: formulas that quietly disagree with the official figures. So the extraction script tests itself before it writes a single row. It recomputes 312 sector and activity growth rates from the levels and compares each one with the rate NBS printed. The largest gap across all 312 is zero to five decimal places.</p>
<div class="callout warn"><p>The check did catch one thing, in the NBS tables rather than the model. The printed nominal GDP total for Q4 2024 is ₦10.16 billion higher than the 46 activities it is meant to sum, and higher than NBS's own 2024 annual total implies. It is 0.01% of GDP and moves one figure on the dashboard, Q4 2025 nominal growth, which reads 17.56% against 17.55% printed. I would not have seen it if I had copied the published totals instead of rebuilding them.</p></div>

<p>The dumbbell is a custom visual I wrote in TypeScript and D3 and packaged as a <code>.pbiviz</code>. It sorts by the size of the move, can flip its colours for measures where lower is better, draws any change that rounds to zero at the chosen precision as no change, and supports cross filtering, keyboard navigation and high contrast themes. Its data layer has 27 unit tests.</p>
<p>The rest of the report is generated too. One script builds the data model, the 36 measures, the three pages and the theme, in Nigerian flag green with an original logo. When NBS publishes the Q3 numbers I can point the script at the new workbook and run it again.</p>

<section class="sec"><div class="fids"><span class="n">07</span><span class="t">What to take from it</span><span class="r"></span></div>
<div class="closer"><div class="q">The headline held steady while the sectors under it traded places</div>
<p>Nigeria's 4.43% is real, and it is an improvement. It is also the net result of a power sector that went into contraction, an oil sector that cooled sharply, and a non-oil economy that did enough to cover both. If you only watch the headline, it looks like nothing happened this year. What actually happened is that the sectors that led in 2025 fell back and a different group, mostly farming and telecoms, took over. I think that is worth more attention than the 0.20 point rise.</p></div>
