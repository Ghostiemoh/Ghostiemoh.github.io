<h3 class="nh">Halved in a year, then nine months at 15%</h3>
<p class="lede">Nigeria's consumer price inflation peaked at 34.8% in December 2024. Twelve months later it was 15.15%. Then it stopped falling. From January to August 2026 the rate never left a band between 15.06% and 15.93%, before easing to 14.63% in September.</p>
<p>I built a Power BI dashboard on all nine NBS consumer price releases for 2026 to find out why the fall stopped. The short answer is that two things happened at the same time and cancelled each other out.</p>

<h3 class="nh">Core inflation kept falling. Food turned around</h3>

<section class="sec"><div class="fids"><span class="n">07</span><span class="t">How it was built</span><span class="r"></span></div>
<h3 class="nh">Nine releases, one cumulative workbook, and checks before every step</h3>
<p>The source is the nine NBS CPI releases for January to September 2026, each a zip holding the PDF report and an Excel workbook. The September workbook turns out to be cumulative: every month back to 1995 on the 2024 base, for 24 price series, nationally and separately for urban and rural Nigeria. Only the state table is published one month at a time, so each release contributes that.</p>
<p>Before anything reached Power BI, the extraction script checked itself. Each release's own figures match the September workbook, so NBS has revised nothing. The 198 year-on-year and month-on-month rates derived from the indexes match the rates NBS printed exactly. And the thirteen spending categories rebuild the headline index they are meant to add up to. In the finished dashboard, 54 live values across every month of 2026 match that month's own release.</p>
<div class="callout warn"><p>Three traps would have silently dropped months. Older workbooks store months as real Excel dates while newer ones use text, 2025 says "Jun" where 2026 says "June", and the March release arrives in the legacy <code>.xls</code> format.</p></div>

<section class="sec"><div class="fids"><span class="n">08</span><span class="t">What to take from it</span><span class="r"></span></div>
<div class="closer"><div class="q">The headline is stuck because food is</div>
<p>Nigeria's inflation looks stuck at 15%, but most of what sits underneath it is still moving down. Core inflation is cooling steadily, every spending category is lower than a year ago, and the monthly rise fell close to zero in September. What is holding the headline up is food, and right now food inflation is mostly a southern story. If the coming releases show food cooling the way core already has, the plateau should break. Until then the national figure hides a 42 point spread between the states where food is hottest and coolest.</p></div>
